Course description

This course develops the fundamental ideas and techniques of modern real analysis, with primary emphasis on functions defined on Euclidean spaces.

The Fall quarter begins with a brief review of the necessary point-set ideas and then develops Lebesgue measure, measurable sets and functions, and Lebesgue integration. Particular emphasis is placed on the Monotone Convergence Theorem, Fatou's Lemma, and the Dominated Convergence Theorem, together with the relationships among pointwise convergence, almost-everywhere convergence, convergence in measure, and convergence in L1.

Course perspective. Fall 2026 and Winter 2027 are intended as a coherent two-quarter sequence focused on real analysis in Euclidean spaces. Systematic extensions to general measure spaces may be treated subsequently.

Tentative weekly outline

Three one-hour lectures per week. The first lecture is an overview. The schedule may be adjusted as the course develops.

Week 1 · Overview and preliminaries
  • Lecture 1: Overview of real analysis and the motivation for Lebesgue theory
  • Essential set theory and countability
  • Euclidean topology, compactness, and Borel sets
Week 2 · Outer measure
  • Lebesgue outer measure on Euclidean space
  • Basic properties of outer measure
  • Carathéodory measurability
Week 3 · Lebesgue measurable sets
  • Elementary properties of measurable sets
  • Borel sets and null sets
  • Translation, scaling, and regularity ideas
Week 4 · Structure and approximation of measurable sets
  • Approximation by open and closed sets
  • Cantor-type examples and sets of measure zero
  • Behavior under transformations
Week 5 · Measurable functions
  • Equivalent characterizations
  • Algebra of measurable functions
  • Limits, suprema, infima, limsup and liminf
Week 6 · Approximation and almost-everywhere ideas
  • Simple functions
  • Almost-everywhere equality
  • Egorov-type approximation and related ideas
Week 7 · Lebesgue integral and monotone convergence
  • Integral of simple and nonnegative measurable functions
  • Monotone Convergence Theorem
  • Applications
Week 8 · Fatou and dominated convergence
  • Fatou's Lemma
  • Integrable functions and positive/negative parts
  • Dominated Convergence Theorem
Week 9 · Modes of convergence
  • Convergence in L1, in measure, and almost everywhere
  • Relations among modes of convergence
  • Counterexamples and applications
Week 10 · Applications and synthesis
  • Comparison with Riemann integration
  • Parameter-dependent integrals and convergence arguments
  • Review and transition to Winter quarter
